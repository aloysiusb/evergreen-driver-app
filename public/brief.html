The driver app — ready for you to try

What it does, what you can change yourselves, and how to test it properly before anyone depends on it.

Prepared by Virginia Payson For Alyssa & Dave 20 September 2026

In the truck

  • Every stop for the day on the phone, in sheet order, with the sheet's own colours and section headers
  • One tap marks a stop done and writes it straight back to the sheet
  • Work survives a dead zone. Marking a stop done with no signal used to quietly undo itself a few seconds later. It now waits on the phone and saves the moment signal returns — nothing lost, nothing to remember.
  • One tap reports a problem — contamination, overflow, skipped, photo. A second tap says how much: 1 bag, 2 bags, 3+ bags. No typing at all.
  • Photos from the kerb. A driver photographs the bin, and a link lands in a new Photos per Notes column in the route sheet.
  • Photos are shrunk on the phone before they are sent. A modern phone photo is three to five megabytes; these leave the phone at around 60–120 KB. On one bar of signal at the far end of a route that is the difference between a driver waiting at the bin and not noticing it happened — and it keeps the data cost down. They are still easily clear enough to show what is wrong with a bin.

Dave — the buttons are yours to set

This is the part I'd most like your opinion on. The words on those buttons are the words that end up in the sheet, and you know what actually goes wrong on a route better than I do.

https://evergreen-driver-app.onrender.com/admin

It will ask for a code the first time — the one in the email, not the driver one. Then scroll to Incident buttons.

What you can change there:

  • The wording on each of the four buttons. Whatever you type appears on the button and is what gets written into Driver Notes — they can't say different things. "Wrong stuff in bin" instead of "Contamination", if that's what the crew actually says.
  • The second row of choices that appears after a button is pressed. Contamination is currently 1 bag, 2 bags, 3+ bags, Recycling, Non-compost, Other. Type your own list, separated by commas.
  • A third row, if a route needs more detail. Leave it blank and it's skipped — one tap still files a complete report.
  • The colours, and how big the buttons are.
  • Whether the camera button lights up after a report, as a reminder. It doesn't open the camera on its own.

Nothing needs a developer. Change it, press Save & Push, and every driver's phone updates within seconds — no reinstalling, no reloading. "Other" opens a typing box, for the days the lists don't fit.

Alyssa — the dispatcher board

The screen to leave open. It's meant to replace ringing round to ask how things are going.

From the trucks

  • Every route at once — stops done, total stops, and a progress bar for each, updating as drivers tap
  • The current stop on each route, by customer name
  • A live feed of every incident, note and photo as it happens, each with the customer's name and address
  • Photos appear as pictures, not links — you can see what's at the kerb from your desk

And from the website, on the same screen

  • A new customer request appears the moment someone submits it — their name, their address, and that it's waiting in the queue
  • Holds, missed pickups and account changes come through the same way
  • Approving or declining one updates that entry in place rather than adding another line

And it remembers the day

  • The feed survives a reload — refresh at three in the afternoon and the morning is still there
  • A day picker shows any of the last seven days, so "what happened on Tuesday" no longer means opening the spreadsheet
  • A past day is clearly labelled, and today's events don't quietly slide into it

Also

  • A light/dark switch, top right — it remembers which you chose
  • If a route ever can't be saved to, a warning names that route. That failure used to be invisible until a driver said their work had vanished.
  • Anything done in test mode is labelled "Test drive" and never moves the real stop counts — so showing someone the app can't make your numbers wrong

Try it without a truck

https://evergreen-driver-app.onrender.com/driver/tues-dv/?sim=1
  • Tap 🗺 Map, then ▶ Drive. The truck follows the real route.
  • Back on Stops, tap Contamination on any stop, then 2 bags.
  • Tap 📶 Signal to switch it off, mark a stop done, and watch it say waiting for signal. Switch it back on and it saves itself. That's the one worth seeing.

Open the dispatcher board in a second window and you'll see everything arrive there as you go. Nothing you do in test mode touches the real route sheet.

Testing it for real — three stages

The desk test proves the app works. It can't prove it works in a truck, because everything it does with GPS, signal and a camera is simulated there. Please treat the first real run as a test, not as the new way of working.

1 · At a desk 15 minutes, either of you

The test drive above. Nothing to lose, and nothing reaches the sheet.

2 · On a phone, parked 15 minutes, Dave

This is the part a computer can't check. On an actual phone:

  • Take a photo of something. Does the camera open, and does the picture appear on the dispatcher board?
  • Tap the buttons wearing whatever you normally wear. Are they big enough with gloves on?
  • Add it to your home screen (browser menu → Add to Home Screen) and open it from there.
  • Mark a stop done and undo it, and check the sheet agrees.

Anything awkward here is worth saying — the wording, sizes and colours are all adjustable.

3 · One real route, one day the real test

Please don't let the app be the only record on the first day. Keep whatever you'd normally do running alongside it.

Pick one route, ideally a shorter one, and watch three things:

  • The arrival alert — does it fire once per stop as you pull up, or repeatedly? It used to repeat every twelve seconds. That's fixed, but it has never run at real driving speed.
  • A dead zone — when signal drops, does the banner say work is waiting, and does it clear when signal returns? This is the most important one: it's been tested by simulating a clean cut, never by a real hole in coverage.
  • The camera — do photos reach the board and the sheet from out on the route?

Alyssa — watch the dispatcher board while that route runs. If it goes quiet while the driver says they're working, that is exactly what we need to know.

What's worth reporting

Anything slow, wrong, awkward or surprising — and above all, anything that looked like it saved but didn't. That matters more than any missing feature.

Customers through the website — where this is heading

The other half of the project, and closer than it looks.

What already works

  • A customer submits a request on the website → it lands in a queue, and the office gets an email
  • It appears on the dispatcher board at the same moment
  • You approve it, and the customer is written straight onto the route sheet — right tab, right columns, no retyping
  • If that write fails, the request is not marked approved, so nobody can be quietly lost between the two

The one missing piece

The website has forms for hold, missed pickup, update details and pay an invoice — but no "start service" form. So the new-customer path above is built and tested, and nothing ever reaches it.

That one form is the whole remaining gap between someone finding you online and their bin appearing on a driver's screen.

After that, in rough order

  • Suggest the collection day from the address, since the app already knows where every route goes — still your decision, just not worked out by hand
  • Warn on duplicate addresses, so one property can't quietly end up on two routes
  • Confirmation emails to customers — needs a verified sending domain first, a one-time setup. The office notification already works.
  • Pay online — the QuickBooks groundwork is in place; finishing it would let a customer settle an invoice from the portal instead of being chased

Every one of those removes a retyping step, not a decision. You'd still approve each customer.
