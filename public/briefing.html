What's now live for drivers and dispatch

All of this is deployed and working today. Drivers need no install and no password — they open a link and tap their route.

🧭
Turn-by-turn navigation with the route pre-loaded
Street-level view that rotates with the road, live ETA, and satellite view for spotting driveways and bin placement before pulling up. Drivers can hand off to Google Maps, Waze, or Apple Maps.
📍
Arrival detection
Within 80 metres of a stop the app asks "Arrived?" — one tap marks it done and advances. Less fumbling with a phone while working.
🚦
Traffic-aware routing with reroute prompts
Shows whether traffic is light, moderate, or heavy and how many minutes it's costing. If conditions shift enough to save 8+ minutes, the driver is offered a faster order — it never reroutes on its own.
📋
Two-tap incident reporting
Contamination, overflow, skip, or photo — each drills down to specifics (what kind, how much, why) and writes a structured note straight to the sheet. Replaces the free-text notes we've been keeping by hand.
📡
Dispatcher dashboard
Every route's progress, the stop each driver is on, and incidents filed today — all on one page, updating live. Nothing to refresh.

03

The strategic read on Robert

Robert is new in the seat and has told us plainly that Aspen One intends to "centralize and standardize" waste and composting. From a Head of Sourcing that usually means one of two things: consolidate to fewer vendors on better terms, or run a competitive process.

Six informal handshake arrangements is the most exposed version of where we could be standing when that happens. The good news is that Virginia reached out before he built his strategy — we have a chance to shape the standard rather than respond to it.

The trap to avoid

If the conversation becomes "justify $2,460 a month," we are competing on a national hauler's terms. A large competitor can bundle trash, recycling and compost and show a lower headline number.

The ground to fight on instead

Aspen One's brand rests on climate credibility, and their sustainability team has to publish diversion figures. Nobody is currently capturing the tonnage we divert for them. If we become the vendor supplying auditable diversion data per property, we stop being a line item and become part of their reporting infrastructure — which is considerably harder to swap out.

The upside nobody has named yet

Six properties is a fraction of Aspen One. Snowmass, Highlands, Aspen Mountain dining, Limelight Snowmass and the rest of the Nell's outlets are not on our list. A consolidation conversation is the natural moment to ask which locations belong in scope — and we are the only bidder who can say the routes already run past most of them.
