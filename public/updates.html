Getting between them

Every internal screen now carries the same bar across the top — the Evergreen name on the left, and Routes and Dispatch on the right. A driver on a route also has a ⌂ home button and a 📡 button straight to the dispatcher board.

1 · In the truck

The route lives in the Google Sheet the office already keeps. The app reads it and writes back the moment a stop is marked done. Nothing here replaces the sheet.

Pick a route

Working

Nine routes on one screen, color-coded and labeled by day — Tuesday Valley, 4WD Action, Wednesday Residential, Commercial, Upper Valley, Com/Eagle, Friday, Monday, Max Routes. Tap one to open the day.

The home screen at /

The day's stops, in sheet order

Working

Every stop with account name, address and city. Valley Residential runs to about 210 stops and they all load. Order comes from the sheet, so whatever the office arranges is what the driver sees.

A route page

Row colors and section headers carry through

Working

A row highlighted in the sheet appears highlighted in the app, in the same color. Section headers — rows with no street number — are detected and shown as dividers, keeping their color too. Text switches between black and white automatically so it stays readable on any highlight.

Automatic — taken from the sheet

Notes and access details on every stop

Working

The office's notes ride along with the stop: gate codes, where the bin sits, which dog to call about. Urgent notes show red, holds show amber, and driving directions appear as a separate key chip.

On each stop card

Mark done, and undo

Working

One large button per stop — easy with gloves. Completed stops collapse into a section at the bottom and can be reopened if tapped by mistake. The progress bar and count update as the day goes.

The ✓ on each stop card

Work survives a dead zone

Working

A stop marked done with no signal stays done. The change is held on the phone, a banner says how many are waiting, and they go out the moment signal returns — including after the app has been closed and reopened. Repeated edits to the same stop collapse to the final state, so nothing arrives out of order.

held in the phone's own storage · retried on reconnect, on return to the app, and every 30s

Add a note from the truck

Working

✎ Add a note on any stop writes back to the sheet — a new gate code, a blocked bin, anything the office should know. Notes are queued the same way if there's no signal.

What is already on the stop is shown but cannot be typed over. Adding a note appends to whatever is there, so a stop reads as a line of history and nothing the office wrote can be lost by a driver adding to it.

The note field on any stop card

Report an incident in one tap

Working

Four buttons sit on every stop card — 🚫 contamination, 📦 overflow, ⏭ skipped and 📷 photo. One press files it against that stop and writes it into the Driver Notes column of the sheet. No typing. A driver who wants to add detail can still type a note as well, and both land together.

Tapping one files it and opens a second set of buttons in the same place — for contamination that is 1 bag · 2 bags · 3+ bags · Recycling · Non-compost · Other. Tap one and the note becomes "Contamination: 2 bags". Tap nothing and walk away and the plain report still stands. One tap to file, two to say how much.

Every word of that is yours to change, including a third level if a route needs one. Whatever you type becomes the label on the button and the words written into the sheet, so the two can never say different things. Drivers know the nuances of what actually goes wrong on a route — "Wrong stuff in bin" instead of "Contamination", or whatever fits — and this is the knob for it. Leave a level blank and it is skipped.

Other opens a typing box, for the day the lists do not fit.

Any stop card, on a route page · wording at /admin → Incident buttons

Photograph a stop

Working

The 📷 button opens the phone's rear camera straight to the shutter. The photo is shrunk on the phone before it is sent — a phone photo is three to five megabytes, and these land around 60–120 KB, so it finishes uploading before the driver has put the phone away even on one bar of signal.

The camera opens by itself for contamination and overflow, so nobody has to remember. It is offered again at each step of the drill-down for anyone who waved it away — but a stop that already has a photo is never asked twice.

Each photo gets a new column in the route sheet, "Photos per Notes", holding a link you can click from the spreadsheet. The column is created the first time a photo is taken on a tab. Several photos of one stop stack up in the same cell. The note itself just says 📷 Photo rather than filling up with web addresses.

Photos are stored by the app itself and named so they make sense when opened — 2026-09-20 0346 · Carbondale Family Dental · Tues DV row 9.jpg — not by a number.

📷 on any stop card, and inside every drill-down · links land in column "Photos per Notes"

Arrival alerts, three ways

Working

Within 80 meters of the next stop the phone alerts and asks — one tap marks it done and moves on. Set per phone: sound (a soft two-note chime plus a buzz), silent (buzz only), or off. It fires once per stop, and re-arms only after the driver has genuinely left.

Set per phone, on the map tab

The screen stays awake

Working

No waking the phone with wet or gloved hands between stops. Releases automatically when the app is put away, so it costs no battery once the driver is done.

Automatic while a route is open

Start My Day backup

Working

One tap duplicates the day's tab in the sheet before any changes, so there's always an untouched copy. Once per route per day. "View Original" shows that copy if anyone needs to check what the day started as.

Bottom of a route page

Installs like an app

Working

Add to Home Screen on a phone or tablet and it opens full screen with no browser chrome. It's still a website — nothing to install from a store, nothing to update.

The browser's Share or menu → Add to Home Screen

2 · The map

We deliberately do not write a navigation system. There's no way for a website to give voice guidance with the screen off, so anything built here would be a worse Google Maps. The app owns the route and the proof of work; Google owns the driving.

Hand the run to Google Maps

Working

One tap sends the next nine stops as a single route to the app the driver already knows. On a 200-stop day that replaces 200 round trips between two apps. Waze and Apple Maps are offered too, though those take one destination at a time — their limitation, not ours.

uses stop coordinates rather than typed addresses, so there's no geocoding to get wrong

The map rides with the driver

Needs a real drive

Street-level map that follows the truck. Panning by hand steps out of follow so it doesn't fight the driver; one tap resumes.

Needs a real drive

Satellite and Street View

Working

Aerial imagery for spotting driveways, gates and bin placement before pulling up, and Street View to scout an unfamiliar property. The plain street map stays the default — it's the clearest to read while moving.

Map tab

Working

Planned follows the sheet. Live reorders the remaining stops for current traffic and says how many minutes it saves. It never reorders on its own — if conditions shift enough to matter it offers, and the driver decides.

3 · The office

Dispatcher dashboard

Working

Every route on one screen, updating live as drivers work — stops done against stops total, progress per route, which stop each driver is on, and any incidents filed today. Nothing to refresh: it updates as it happens. This is the screen to leave open during a run.

evergreen-driver-app.onrender.com/dispatch

Photos appear on the board as they are taken

Working

A driver's photo shows up in the live feed as the picture itself, not a link to one — with the customer's name, the address, and what was reported. No refresh, no opening anything.

That is the point of it: the office can see what is actually at the curb without phoning to ask.

/dispatch

Each route in its own color

Working

A route's color is the same everywhere it appears — the driver's home screen and the dispatcher board read the same setting, so a route is recognizable at a glance without reading its name. On the board the color fills the whole card header. Routes that aren't running today stay visible but step back.

Every one of those colors is yours to change, along with the board's own light and dark schemes.

/admin → Route cards, and Dispatcher board

Request queue

Working

Holds, missed pickups, account changes and new-customer enquiries from the portal, in one screen to approve or decline. Approving a new customer appends them to the route tab you choose. Approving a hold simply marks it handled — it doesn't put anyone on a route. A request can't be decided twice, and if the sheet write fails the request stays pending rather than being marked done and lost.

/approval · filters by type · updates live as requests arrive

Email on every new request

Working

Name, address, contact details, what they asked for, and a link straight to the queue. The recipient is a setting rather than something baked in, so it can be handed over without a code change, and it can be switched off.

Automatic — sent to the office

Style editor

Working
/admin · or the 🎨 button on the dispatcher board

Colors, typefaces, text sizes and corner rounding for the whole driver app, with a live preview. Changes save centrally and push to every driver's phone immediately — no reinstall, no waiting. Includes a set of ten typefaces and a reset.

/admin

Error log

Working

The app reports its own failures rather than failing quietly. Identical problems are counted rather than repeated, so one fault appears as a single line with a number beside it. It has already caught a live bug that had been hidden for months.

/errors

A new sheet can't shift the columns

Working

The app reads each tab's own header row to find which column is which, rather than assuming fixed positions. Add a column, remove one, or reorder them, and it follows. If a header can't be read at all it refuses to write rather than guessing — the driver's work waits safely on the phone until the sheet is readable again, which is the one behavior that cannot lose anything.

The header words are what matter, not the positions. Renaming Driver Notes to something else will pause writes to that tab until it is put back.

Automatic · confirm any new sheet at /api/sheet-check

Sheet checker

Working

Point it at any spreadsheet and it says whether the app can open it, which tabs it recognizes, which it will ignore, and what the columns look like — before switching over, rather than finding out from a driver.

Not switched on

A shared code protecting the route data. Drivers tap a link once and stay signed in for a year — no login screen in the cab. A separate code protects the style editor and the error log. Until it's set, customer names, addresses and gate codes are readable by anyone with the link.

Testing it for real

The desk test proves the app works. It cannot prove it works in a truck — everything it does with GPS, signal and a camera is simulated there. So the first real run should be treated as a test, not as the new way of working.

1 · At a desk

15 minutes

The test drive above. Nothing to lose and nothing reaches the sheet. Either of you, on a laptop.

2 · On a phone, parked

15 minutes

This is the part a computer cannot check. On an actual phone: take a photo and see whether it reaches the dispatcher board; tap the buttons wearing work gloves; add it to the home screen and open it from there; mark a stop done and undo it, and check the sheet agrees.

3 · One real route, one day

The real test

Keep the normal process running alongside it. The app should not be the only record on the first day.

Pick one route, ideally a shorter one, and watch three things:

The arrival alert — does it fire once as you pull up, or repeatedly? It used to repeat every twelve seconds. That is fixed, but it has never run at real driving speed.

A dead zone — when signal drops, does the banner say work is waiting, and does it clear when signal returns? This is the most important one: it has been tested by simulating a clean cut, never by a real hole in coverage, which is messier.

The camera — do photos reach the board and the sheet from out on the route, rather than from a desk?

Someone reachable, and the usual paperwork still running

Watch the board while that route runs

Working

Stops should complete, and incidents and photos should arrive, without anyone asking for them. If the board goes quiet while the driver says they are working, that is exactly the thing worth reporting.

What is worth reporting

6 · What was wrong

These were all running in the live app. None of them showed an error message — that is precisely why they lasted as long as they did.

01

A driver's work could vanish

Marking a stop done without signal showed a tick, then quietly undid it three seconds later. The stop was neither done on the phone nor in the sheet, and nothing recorded that it had ever happened. The most expensive of these by a distance.

02

The route line never drew on a real route

The map asked Google for directions through all 210 stops at once; Google accepts 25. Every request was rejected, which also silently killed the drive-time estimate, the Live reordering and the traffic prompt — four features, none of them working, no error anywhere.

03

A crash hiding behind that one

Fixing the above let the code run for the first time in months — and revealed a crash where a rural road has no traffic data. Caught by the new error log within hours rather than never.

04

Updates weren't reaching drivers

A fix could be deployed and drivers would keep running the old version for up to a day, with no way for anyone to tell.

05

Three of four customer forms were pretend

"Request hold", "Missed pickup" and "Update info" showed a green tick and recorded nothing. The portal also displayed an invented customer's name and address to whoever opened it.

06

The arrival alert repeated

It re-fired every twelve seconds while a driver was parked at a stop. Survivable as a silent message; not once it buzzes the phone.

07

Saved data was wiped on every update

The request queue and settings lived inside the container rather than on the attached disk, so deploying erased them. Now fixed and verified.

What's next — customers through the website

The aim is that an enquiry turns into a stop on a route without anyone retyping it. Most of that machinery is already built and running; the piece that is missing is smaller than it sounds.

What already works

Working

A request arriving from the website lands in a queue, the office gets an email, and approving it writes the customer straight onto the route sheet — into the right columns on the right tab. Nothing is retyped, and if the sheet write fails the request is not marked approved, so a customer can never be quietly lost between the two.

/portal → /approval

The missing piece: a sign-up form

Not built yet

The portal today offers hold, missed pickup, update details and pay an invoice. There is no "start service" form — yet the queue, the approval screen and the sheet-writing behind it all already handle new customers and are waiting for it.

That is the next thing to build, and it is the smallest remaining step to a new customer going from the website to a driver's screen without anyone typing anything.

Would live at /portal

Suggesting the right day

Proposed

The app already knows where every route goes. A new address could be matched against them and the likely collection day offered to the office as a suggestion — still approved by a person, but not worked out by hand each time.

Catching duplicates

Proposed

Warn when an address already appears on a route, so the same property does not quietly end up on two.

Telling the customer

Needs a sending domain

Confirmation emails to customers need a verified sending domain before anything reaches them — a once-only setup. The office notification already works. After that: a welcome message saying what goes in the bin, and which day to put it out.

Paying online

Proposed

The groundwork for connecting to QuickBooks is in place. Finishing it would let a customer settle an invoice from the portal rather than being chased.

The shape of it

Every one of these removes a retyping step, not a decision. The office still approves each customer; the app just stops making anyone copy things from one screen to another.
