Diversion and emissions impact

Aspen One's public sustainability reporting depends on auditable diversion figures. The estimates below are derived from contracted service volumes — we would replace them with measured weights under a formal agreement.

Methodology & assumptions

These figures are volume-derived estimates, not measured weights. Stated plainly so they can be audited and challenged:

  • 95-gallon cart of source-separated organics ≈ 200 lbs at typical fill
  • 3-yard dumpster of organics ≈ 900 lbs at ~65% average fill
  • Calculated at peak-season frequency across 12 months, un-discounted for off-season reductions — actual tonnage is likely lower
  • Emissions avoided uses the EPA WARM differential for food waste composted vs. landfilled, ≈ 0.58 MTCO₂e per short ton

Recommendation: installing scale data at the Little Nell and Buttermilk — the two highest-volume sites, together roughly 74% of estimated tonnage — would convert these estimates into reportable, third-party-defensible figures for Aspen One's sustainability disclosures.

At approximately 179 short tons diverted annually, the avoided emissions of roughly 104 metric tons CO₂e is comparable to taking about 23 passenger vehicles off the road for a year. That figure belongs in Aspen One's reporting — and right now no one is capturing it.

03

Verified service delivery

Every collection is now logged by the driver in the field, in real time. This addresses the most common failure in commercial waste contracts: disputed service.

Timestamped, GPS-confirmed collection records

Drivers mark each stop complete on-site. The record carries the time and the confirmed location — not a route sheet reconciled at end of day.

Why it matters: when a property manager reports a missed pickup, the answer is a record rather than a disagreement.
Contamination reporting with photo documentation

When a driver finds non-compostable material, they file a structured report on the spot — material type, severity, and a photograph — tied to that specific property and date.

Why it matters: contamination data by site shows exactly which kitchens need staff training. That is operational intelligence Aspen One would otherwise pay a consultant to gather.
Live operational visibility

Service status across all properties is visible as it happens, rather than reconstructed from paperwork at month end.

Why it matters: a single reporting standard across every Aspen One property — which is precisely what a centralization effort requires.
