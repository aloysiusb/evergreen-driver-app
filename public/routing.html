03

What the numbers say

Wednesday Commercial is the one genuinely inefficient route
It carries 65 stops across five towns with no geographic anchor — only 46% sit in its largest town. The truck travels from Glenwood Springs through Carbondale and Basalt to Aspen, roughly 45 miles each way, for fewer stops than the Friday Aspen run alone.
So: Aspen's Monday/Wednesday/Friday customers get a tight, efficient run twice a week and a valley-long haul the third time. Same customers, same service promised, very different cost to deliver.
Two trucks are in Glenwood Springs on the same day
Weds DV Res services 197 Glenwood stops on Wednesday. Weds Commercial separately sends a truck to Glenwood for 3 stops — 1410 Grand Avenue, 1405 Grand Avenue, and 3799 Highway 82 — on that same Wednesday.
So: this is the clearest single fix on the board. Three stops absorbed into a route already working that town.
COMEAGLE is a different business, not a bad route
28 stops spanning Vail, Avon, Edwards, Eagle, Glenwood, New Castle and Rifle — the widest spread on the board by a distance. But the stop list is institutional and sticky: four City Markets, two Colorado Mountain College campuses, Town of Avon, Avon Police, Vail Mountain School, and Eagle River Water & Sanitation at two sites.
So: the question isn't whether to cut it. It's whether Eagle County is a market worth building density in, or a corridor to be priced for what it actually costs. Right now it's being run as neither.
Monday and Friday carry a handful of long detours
Monday's Aspen run includes two Redstone stops and two in Glenwood. Friday's carries single stops in Glenwood, El Jebel and Basalt. Each is a substantial detour off an otherwise tight Aspen route.
So: either move them onto a route that already passes them, or recognise that a single stop 40 miles off-route is not priced like one.
The residential routes are already doing it right
Tues DV (480 stops, one town), Thurs UV (251, one town), Weds DV Res (210, 94% one town) and Tues 4WD (229, 87% Basalt) are textbook — dense, compact, anchored.
So: the pattern to copy already exists in-house. The commercial routes are the exception, not the rule.
04

Recommended changes

A
Break Wednesday Commercial into geographic legs

Make Wednesday mirror Monday and Friday: a clean Aspen commercial run. Redistribute the rest to routes already working those towns that day.

→3 Glenwood stops join Weds DV Res, which is already in Glenwood with 197 stops that day
→30 Carbondale stops form a Carbondale commercial leg, or ride with Max Routes
→27 Aspen stops become a standalone Wednesday Aspen commercial run
→Basalt and El Jebel stops move to a mid-valley leg or absorb into Tuesday

The prize: Aspen's three-times-weekly commercial customers — including The Little Nell and the Residences — get identical service quality all three days instead of twice.

B
Decide what Eagle County is

COMEAGLE needs a deliberate answer, not continued drift. Two honest options:

→Grow it. The anchor accounts are strong. Target density in Vail, Avon and Edwards until stops-per-mile matches the valley routes.
→Price it honestly. If it stays at 28 stops, the rate should reflect a 200-mile day, not valley economics.
C
Sweep the strays off Monday and Friday

Redstone, Glenwood, El Jebel and Basalt singles on otherwise-Aspen routes. Move each to the route that already drives past it, or price the detour into the account.

D
Hold the line: never route across the whole valley

The operating rule the good routes already follow — a route is a segment of the valley, not a traverse of it. When a new account doesn't fit an existing segment, that's a signal about pricing or about which day it belongs on, not a reason to stretch a route.

E
Let the app measure the result

The driver app now reports both the planned route time and the traffic-optimised alternative on every run. Recording that gap per route for a month shows which routes are genuinely inefficient versus which only look untidy on a spreadsheet — and whether these changes actually delivered.

05

How to verify all of this yourself

None of the above should be taken on trust. Every figure comes from the route sheet and can be checked in a few minutes.

Check it in the sheet

  1. Route sizes: open each route tab and count rows with a street address in the Address column. Section headers and blank rows don't count.
  2. Concentration: in any route tab, filter the City column and compare counts. Weds Commercial should show Carbondale 30, Aspen 27, Basalt 4, Glenwood 3, El Jebel 1.
  3. The Glenwood overlap: search Weds Commercial for Glenwood — three rows near the top. Then open Weds DV Res, same day, and see how many Glenwood rows it carries.
  4. COMEAGLE spread: the tab runs Vail → Avon → Edwards → Eagle → Glenwood → New Castle → Rifle, starting from the Carbondale yard. Map those against each other.
  5. Monday and Friday strays: filter each for any city that isn't Aspen.

One known data issue found while checking: The Little Nell is entered as 675 Durrant Street — misspelled and missing the avenue. Worth correcting, since it makes the property hard to find when searching our own records.
